Graduation Project — Graded Excellent (A)

Urban Readiness Requirements for Regional Competitiveness Zones

A strategic regional planning study that transforms urban readiness into a practical roadmap for competitive regional development across the Asyut – Qena – El Quseir – Hurghada regional corridor.

Assiut · Qena · Qusier · Al-Ghurdaqah, Egypt 2026 — 2027 Faculty of Urban &Regional Planning, Cairo University
Prologue Why this region

Asyut – Qena – El Quseir – Hurghada regional corridor holds enormous latent competitiveness — but readiness is not distributed evenly. Some settlements are one investment away from a step change. Others are structurally excluded from the systems that would let them grow.

This study set out to make that unevenness visible, measurable, and actionable — treating “urban readiness” not as a slogan, but as a spatial variable that can be mapped, modelled, and planned for.

Method Research to Strategy

Three moves from raw geography to planning strategy

01

Reading the Region

The study opens not with a plan, but with a question: which parts of this region are already prepared to compete, and which are held back by infrastructure, geography, or policy gaps? Thematic GIS maps and base data were assembled across the Assiut–Qena–Qusier–Al-Ghurdaqah corridor to build a shared spatial understanding.

02

Modelling Readiness

A series of spatial and statistical analyses — hotspot detection, network connectivity, and Thiessen service modelling — were layered to build a composite picture of regional readiness, translating raw geography into a legible index of opportunity and constraint.

03

Strategic Translation

Analytical outputs were translated into a roadmap: a set of strategic planning recommendations prioritizing where readiness could be built fastest, and where longer-term investment in connectivity and services would unlock regional competitiveness.

Analysis Selected Spatial Analyses

Three analytical lenses on the same territory

To establish a reliable basis for planning decisions, a series of GIS-based analyses examined spatial patterns, connectivity, and regional relationships within the study area — each surfacing a different layer of development opportunity and constraint.

Getis-Ord Gi*

Hotspot Analysis

Statistically significant clusters of high and low economic activity were identified across the study region, isolating where competitive advantage already concentrates — and where it doesn 't.

Intersection &connectivity modelling

Network Analysis

Inter- and intra-connection nodes were mapped across the settlement network, revealing which centres function as regional hubs and which remain isolated from the wider system.

Voronoi service-area modelling

Thiessen Polygons

Service catchments were generated for each urban centre, exposing gaps in regional coverage and informing where new investment would extend readiness furthest.

Result Strategic Outcome

A roadmap for competitive regional development

A regional-competitiveness roadmap identifying priority development corridors, supported by a full suite of spatial analyses that ground each recommendation in evidence rather than assumption.

The final grade of Excellent (A) reflected not just the technical rigor of the GIS work, but the clarity with which that analysis was translated into planning recommendations — an approach that I intend to carry forward in my future projects.
